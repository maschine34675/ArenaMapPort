# ArenaMapPort 0.3.0

Brings **Bowl**, an open-air stadium arena, from Escape from Tarkov: Arena to SPT, next to **Air Pit**,
**Equator (Day/Night)** and **Bay5**. The scenes of those three maps are those of 0.2.3. What changes on them: the gates and
vehicles of their team spawn enclosures use Arena's own poses and motion, Equator's players spawn inside the booths, and
Equator and Bay5 load several times faster (see Changes).
Like 0.2.3, the maps need an installed **Escape from Tarkov: Arena** (sold separately from Escape from Tarkov):
the server registers them and the client loads them only on a PC with a complete Arena installation.
Also new in 0.3.0: the map bundles are encrypted with a key derived from the files of that Arena installation, and the game
says why the maps are missing when they are (see Changes).

## Included

- **Bowl:** the stadium arena from Arena. The sunken stadium field with a crashed Mi-26 in the middle, containers and
  guard towers, and a gated team pocket at each end (east and west). Like Bay5 and Air Pit it has two normal
  server-time choices, a progressing day/night cycle and dynamic weather. Bot spawns, waves and PMC squads are
  included, and two gates (Bowl BTR Gate and Bowl Army Gate) serve as extractions. The spectator stands are scenery.
- **Air Pit:** an abandoned terminal hangar from Arena. It contains the furnished main hall
  with its four shops, the passport hall and the apron. Like Bay5 it has two normal server-time
  choices, a progressing day/night cycle and dynamic weather. Bot spawns, waves and PMC squads
  are included, and two gates (Air Pit North Gate and Air Pit South Gate) serve as extractions.
- **Equator** with a realistic daytime appearance and an optional neon midnight appearance.
- **Bay5** with normal time progression and dynamic weather.
- Imported geometry, materials, lighting, audio, collision and navigation, plus
  map-specific repairs.
- The bundles of all four maps are encrypted, with one small key file per map in the new `keys` folder
  (see Changes).
- Optional DynamicMaps overview maps for all four maps, stored inside ArenaMapPort's own
  directory. Air Pit and Bowl show their ground floor; their upper spectator areas are omitted.
- Original map descriptions in English, German and Russian, with English fallback.
- Per-room gunshot reverb on all four maps: shots ring differently in a big hall than in a
  small shop. The room data is invented, because neither EFT nor Arena provides gunshot
  reverb values; outdoors the shipped acoustics stay. It can be switched off per map under
  `Audio (<map key>)` / `RoomShotAcoustics` with `General/DeveloperMode` enabled (enable it
  and restart EFT).
- Spawn gates for mode plugins (introduced in 0.2.2): a mode plugin such as ArenaMode can keep the team spawn enclosures
  closed until the fight starts, as Arena does. The gates and vehicles use Arena's own poses and motion (see Changes), and
  the plugin can wait for the moment the gates stand open instead of a fixed time (see Changes).

The maps are intended for **ArenaMode**, which is installed separately. No loot or scav raids
are provided. Tested with **SPT 4.1.6 / EFT 0.16.9.5-40743**; server compatibility is `~4.1.5`.
Optional DynamicMaps integration was tested with 1.2.1.

## Changes since 0.2.3

- **New map: Bowl.** What it carries over from the other maps and what is specific to it:
  - Arena's own content on top of the geometry: projection decals, ballistic surfaces, light culling and
    flicker, small physics props that react when touched, ambient audio and room acoustics. The per-room
    gunshot reverb of the other maps is on here too. A fresh navigation mesh covers the field and both team
    pockets as one connected area.
  - Player spawns sit in the two pockets. Bots spawn on Arena's AI core points and patrol the field along a patrol
    way built from Arena's own patrol points. Each extraction box lies just outside one pocket's gate.
  - The two pockets sit behind gates (the east pocket's gate and BTR, the west pocket's army gate and
    ambulance), open at raid start and registered with the spawn gates, so a mode plugin can hold them closed until its
    fight starts (introduced in 0.2.2); the hold seals both pockets for bots and players and the release opens them again
    (verified with the developer test hold). How the gates look and move is described under "Gates and vehicles" below.
  - The play area is bounded by Arena's invisible border walls, which Bowl keeps because the stadium has no
    other bound. The navigation mesh ends at them.
  - The tunnel tarp uses a stand-in shader: Arena's reflective two-sided shader does not exist in EFT, so the
    tarp shows its cloth texture on both sides without the reflection.
  - The DynamicMaps overview covers the ground floor: the field, both pockets (drawn open), the outline of the
    Mi-26 and the containers. The stands, the Mi-26's roof and the tower platforms are not drawn.
  - Bowl has its own banner, four loading screens (Arena ships no loading screen for Bowl, so these are its four
    main-menu backdrops), its original description in three languages and its own position on the region-selection
    artwork.
- **Gates and vehicles use Arena's own poses and motion.** In 0.2.3 the gates of the team spawn enclosures were switched off when
  they opened (Equator's booth doors slid a measured distance). Arena does not hide them, it moves them, and the parts now take
  Arena's data: the animation clips of Bowl's gate parts and the door script that animates the doors of Equator, Bay5 and Air Pit.
  In an ordinary raid every gate that Arena moves is open from the first moment (Air Pit's passport control excepted, see below;
  the few doors Arena never moves stay shut, see Known limitations). A mode plugin such
  as ArenaMode can still hold the gates closed
  and open them when the fight starts (the calls are unchanged); the parts then move where they used to vanish, over half of
  Arena's authored time, so a release finishes after 1.5 s on Air Pit, 3.5 s on Equator and Bay5 and 6.8 s on Bowl. The developer
  setting `Spawns/SpawnGateDriveScale` (0.1 to 1.0, default 0.5) changes that factor and `Spawns/SpawnGateDrive` off opens the
  gates at once. While a hold is on, the enclosures are still cut out of the bots' navigation, and the navigation of Equator, Bay5
  and Air Pit was rebaked around the new poses.
  - **Bowl:** the BTR has reversed out of its slot and stands at the slot's west end, which closes the gap there as in Arena; the
    ambulance has driven out of its bay and stands at the west pocket's exit; the army gate's two leaves are swung open, and the
    east pocket's gate leaf has slid 3.8 m aside onto its rail. The poses are the end poses of Arena's clips. On a release the
    parts move there over the clips' own durations (BTR 7.7 s, ambulance 8.3 s, army gate 10 s, gate leaf 13.5 s, all halved)
    with a smooth ease instead of Arena's exact curve. Bots do not path through the parked bodies.
  - **Equator:** the ten booth door leaves stand open in their pockets from raid start and, on a release, slide about 1.3 m with the motion
    and timing of Arena's own door script (5 s, one leaf 7 s) instead of the slide of 0.2.3, which was sized from the frame geometry; the two leaves of the extra
    door at the far end of the south booth have no script of their own and use their neighbours' numbers. The FCK shop's roller
    shutter stays shut like in Arena (0.2.3 opened it because the Usec spawn stood behind it).
  - **Bay5:** two of the warehouse's roller doors and the one-man door lift about 3.1 m on Arena's 4 s linear
    motion, and both depot gates swing open on Arena's 7 s curve with their frames and posts standing; the second depot gate
    stayed shut in 0.2.3. The roller door behind the tarp, which 0.2.3 opened, is shut again, like the two east-wall doors behind their
    barricades (see Known limitations).
  - **Air Pit:** in the south team box the three front bus doors fold out to the pose of Arena's back row, which stands open as
    Arena built it (0.2.3 hid all six assemblies); the way out of the box leads through two rows of open doors, with doorways of roughly 1.1 to 1.3 m
    in the back row (worked out from Arena's geometry, not measured in game). Arena has no animation data for the bus doors, so their swing (3 s, halved) is ours and the open pose is
    Arena's. The passport control in the north keeps its rule: closed in every ordinary raid, sealed for bots and the northern
    limit of the play area. A mode plugin's release now turns its 16 turnstile leaves open with Arena's own motion (1.7 s,
    halved), where 0.2.3 switched the leaves and the red lamps off, and the lane lamps flip from the red X to the green arrow in the
    frame the last leaf has opened. The arrows sit on the booths' north face, the side the north team sees.
- **Equator: players spawn inside the booths.** The four player spawn points now stand on Arena's own team-fight markers inside
  the two respawn booths, two per booth, one behind each of its two doors and facing them. In 0.2.3 the Usec spawn stood in the FCK
  shop and the other three on the aprons in front of the doors. A bot spawn point that stood in the FCK hall moved to the open
  ground 7 m in front of the north doors. The two extraction gates were not moved: they stand inside the booths, the edges of
  their boxes 2.6 and 2.9 m from the nearest spawn point. The spawn points are part of the server's database, so the client and the server must come from
  the same ZIP (see Distribution).
- **A done signal for mode plugins.** A mode plugin such as ArenaMode can ask when the gates are open instead of waiting a
  fixed number of seconds per map: `SpawnGates.GatesOpen` is true once no hold is outstanding, no opening is in progress and the
  map's navigation is installed (the carvers that seal the closed gates are gone and those of the parked bodies stand), `IsOpening`
  says a release is still driving, `OpeningSecondsRemaining` gives the longest remaining drive as a timeout, and the event
  `GatesOpened` is raised each time `GatesOpen` turns true (after the ordinary raid-start opening at the navigation install, and at
  the end of a release). A map without gates never reports open. At the default speed a release takes 1.5 s on Air Pit, 3.5 s on
  Equator and Bay5 and 6.8 s on Bowl.
- **Faster Equator and Bay5 loads.** The scene bundles of Equator and Bay5 were built as one compressed block, which Unity unpacked
  completely when it opened the bundle (15 s and 28 s on the test PC). They are rebuilt in blocks of at most
  128 KiB that Unity reads as it needs them, as Air Pit's and Bowl's always were; the content inside is byte-identical. On the
  test PC the scenes bundle now opens within 21 ms (Equator) and 33 ms (Bay5) after the preset bundle, and the whole map load as EFT
  times it dropped from about 19 s to 4.7 s on Equator and from about 32 s to 5.1 s on
  Bay5.
- **Gunshot reverb in steel bodies.** A large thick-steel room, such as Bowl's crashed Mi-26, now gets the metal
  room class (steel walls) instead of the plasterboard room. The rooms of the other three maps keep their classes.
- **Arena installation check on Bowl too.** The check introduced in 0.2.3 (the launcher's registry entry and the
  installation folder verified against the launcher's file list; overrides `General/ArenaInstallPath` and
  `config/arena-install.json`) covers the fourth map like the other three.
- **Messages that say why the Arena maps are missing.** When the maps are not available, the game now shows a message at the main menu
  with the reason and what to do (Arena not found or incomplete, the folder set in `ArenaInstallPath` unusable, the SPT server not offering
  the maps, ArenaMapPort files missing), and after a map failed to load it names the cause (an Arena build the keys do not match, a missing
  or damaged map file). Each message ends with a ref code such as `ref NO_ARENA`; the repository README's "If the maps do not show up" lists them with
  their fixes. The messages are in English or German, following the game's language. The new setting `General/ShowMapNotices` (F12, on by
  default) hides the main-menu message, the "still checking" notice and the reminder in the location selection; it never turns the Arena check off,
  and the message that answers a failed raid start still appears. The server's exact
  reason comes from a new read-only route of the server mod (`/arenamapport/status`: codes only, no path or file content), so the client and
  the server must be the same version: **extract the whole ZIP over the installation, which replaces both DLLs (client and server), with the SPT server closed**,
  because a running server keeps its DLL locked. A server of an older version cannot say why, and the message then asks to reinstall both parts from the same ZIP.
  On the server, the "Arena not found" line in the console is red now and states what to do once instead of twice.
- **Encrypted map bundles.** All twelve bundles (`<map>_scenes`, `<map>_navmesh` and `<map>_preset` of the four
  maps) are encrypted, and the key of each map is derived from the Arena resource files that map is built from: the
  raw texture, mesh and audio data of its scenes (3 to 8 files per map, 218 to
  754 MB), of which the client reads a fixed sample (16 blocks of 64 KiB per file, 3 to 7.4 MB per map). When
  a map is first needed in a session the client reads that sample, derives the key and decrypts the bundle while it
  loads; no decrypted copy is written to disk (the bundle is read through a decrypting stream in memory). The new folder
  `BepInEx/plugins/maschine-ArenaMapPort/keys` holds one small key file per map: it lists the Arena files by path
  and size and the sample it reads, and carries the map's content key wrapped so that only those files open it, never a
  hash of them. Key derivation takes tens of milliseconds per map on the test PC (29 to 58 ms measured, Windows crypto API) and
  decrypting adds up to about 0.9 s to a map load (0.4 to 0.9 s of crypto for the 0.6 to 1 GB scene bundles; the raid loads of Bowl and
  Air Pit, which were measured both ways, stayed within one second of the unencrypted ones).
  - **What it is, and what it is not.** It is a requirement: the bundles are unusable without the Arena files of
    your own PC (the installation check of 0.2.3 still runs first). It is **not copy protection**. The key is tied to
    Arena's files, not to a purchase: anyone who has those files can decrypt the bundles (the format and the tool are
    in the public source), and a shared key or shared decrypted bundles work for everyone. The art and audio in the
    ZIP (loading screens, banners, the region-selection artwork, the acoustics sounds, flare and water textures,
    the map descriptions) and the derived data (DynamicMaps overviews, AI sidecars, shader lists, server data)
    stay plain files; only the bundles with the scenes, meshes, materials, textures and navigation are encrypted.
  - **Which Arena build, and Arena updates.** The keys in this release are built for Arena 0.6.1.0.48206 and also
    open on 0.6.0.0.47752, whose resource files are identical (checked against a 0.6.0 installation; your build is in
    the client log at startup: `Arena: found ...`). Another build works as long as the resource files of a map are
    unchanged: a hotfix like 0.6.0 to 0.6.1, which rebuilt the scene files and kept the texture and mesh data, does not
    lock the maps. If a build changes a map's resource files (new or re-imported textures, a Unity upgrade, moved scene
    numbers), that map's key no longer opens: the client logs one error naming the cause (`[BundleKeys] key for '<map
    key>' unavailable: ...`) and the map's raid does not load, while the maps built from unchanged files keep working,
    because every map has its own key. The server does not check the keys, so the map can still appear in the location
    selection. A later ArenaMapPort package adds the key for the new Arena build and keeps this one's, so a PC that has
    not updated Arena keeps working too; there is nothing to configure. An Arena installation whose resource files were
    modified does not unlock the maps either, even when the sizes are unchanged.
- **Distribution.** The ZIP carries four maps. Bowl's bundles are new and all twelve bundles are encrypted, so
  the bundle files of Equator, Bay5 and Air Pit differ from 0.2.3 although the scenes inside are unchanged (their
  navigation bundles were rebaked for the new gate poses), and the new `keys` folder ships next to `bundles`. Upgrading
  needs the full ZIP (no DLL-only package). Keep the client, the bundles and the server database from the same package:
  an older client cannot load the encrypted bundles, this client needs the `keys` folder that belongs to them, and
  Equator's spawn points are in the server's database (an older database would place the Usec spawn in the FCK hall, which
  this client keeps sealed). Encrypted data does not compress, so the ZIP is about as large as the files inside it,
  roughly 3.6 GB (3.4 GiB).

## Known limitations

- **No grass on Bowl.** Arena's grass for Bowl is a GPU-instanced grass scene, which is not ported.
- **Tree wind is not ported.** The ported scenes carry no tree wind settings, so EFT's wind calculation for
  trees is never set up and Bowl's trees are not animated by it. EFT logs `Invalid windSettings` once
  while a ported map loads; this is harmless, it comes from the same missing settings, and vanilla maps do not
  log it.
- **The stands are scenery.** The spectator stands above the field are empty and not part of the play area.
- **The capture point lies under the Mi-26.** Arena's capture point for this mode sits under the crashed
  helicopter's belly, where there is no walkable ground; where objectives go on Bowl is up to the mode plugin.
- **No lamp flares on Bowl.** Equator and Bay5 draw Arena's original lamp flares. Bowl's only active flare lights
  belong to the distant city backdrop, farther away than the 500 m the flare renderer draws, so none appear.
- **The gates move silently.** Arena's gate and door sounds are not ported, and a gate that is opening pushes nobody aside.
- **Air Pit's passport control opens only through a mode plugin.** In an ordinary raid (no mode plugin) it stays closed
  and remains the northern limit of the play area. A spawn-gate hold that nobody releases opens after five minutes with a
  warning.
- **Air Pit's lamps follow Arena's data.** Arena's booths carry the green arrow on their north face only, so from the hall
  side the red X stays lit after a release; the arrows light on the side the north team sees. The red haze over the lanes
  goes out when the release starts, 0.85 s (at the default speed) before the lamps flip.
- **Three of Bay5's warehouse doors stay shut.** The two east-wall doors have planks and a fence in their
  doorways, the roller door behind the tarp has the tarp and a stack of cartons in front of it, and Arena itself moves none of them; they are walls for players and
  bots.
- **Equator's FCK hall is outside the play area.** Its roller shutter stays shut like in Arena, and the hall behind it is
  cut off from the navigation: it cannot be entered and nobody spawns there.
- **The keys cover Arena 0.6.1.0.48206 and 0.6.0.0.47752.** An Arena update that changes a map's resource files
  locks that map until an updated package carries its key. See "Which Arena build, and Arena updates" under Changes.
- **Slower decryption without Windows' crypto API.** The client decrypts through the Windows crypto API (CNG). Where
  that is unavailable (Wine/Proton, very old Windows) it falls back to slower managed code, logs a warning, and map
  loading takes noticeably longer (not measured: both test PCs use the Windows crypto API).

## Installation

1. Install **Escape from Tarkov: Arena** with the Battlestate Games launcher if it is not installed
   yet. It is a separate purchase from Escape from Tarkov.
2. Close EFT and the SPT server.
3. Extract `maschine-ArenaMapPort-0.3.0.zip` into your SPT installation, merging `BepInEx`
   and `SPT_Runtime`. Install both the client and server components.
4. Start the SPT server, then EFT.

The client folder is `BepInEx/plugins/maschine-ArenaMapPort`; its `keys` subfolder belongs to the release, so keep
it and replace it when you update (a missing or damaged key file is reported as an error in the client log, and the
maps do not load).
The server folder remains `SPT_Runtime/user/mods/ArenaMapPort`.

**Upgrading from 0.2.3 or 0.2.2:** with EFT and the SPT server closed, extract the ZIP over the
installation (Bowl's bundles are new and every bundle is now encrypted and comes with a `keys` folder, so the two
DLLs alone are not enough). Keep `BepInEx/config`. The ZIP replaces the client and the server DLL together, which the new messages
need (the server's reason comes from its own DLL); a running SPT server keeps its DLL locked, so close it first.

**Upgrading from 0.2.1 or 0.2.0:** the same, and the old
`BepInEx/plugins/maschine-ArenaMapPort/material-shaders.json` may be deleted.

**Upgrading from 0.1.0:** with EFT and the SPT server closed, extract the ZIP over the
installation. Keep `BepInEx/config`; the configuration migrations run automatically.

**Upgrading a development build:** move the old `BepInEx/plugins/ArenaMapPort` directory
outside `BepInEx/plugins` before extracting. Keep your `BepInEx/config` settings.
Remove the retired `maschine-ArenaMapDebug.dll` if present, either directly in `plugins`
or inside `plugins/ArenaMapDebug`. The included README covers old database/DynamicMaps cleanup.

F12 exposes `NaturalEquatorNight` to use the natural appearance in the next Equator night raid, `ArenaInstallPath` (the Arena folder
override, empty = automatic) and `ShowMapNotices` (the main-menu messages; they never switch the Arena check off).
`DeveloperMode` is an advanced setting and is off by default.
