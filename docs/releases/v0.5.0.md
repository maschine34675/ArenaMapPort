# ArenaMapPort 0.5.0

7 November 2026. Iceberg, the fifth Arena map, joins Equator, Bay5, Air Pit and Bowl. The spectators can answer a mode's
match events with original crowd reactions, and the crowd murmur is now Arena's neutral in-match recording.

## Changes since 0.4.0

- **Iceberg (new map).** The Iceberg resort hotel from Escape from Tarkov: Arena (Arena's `Arena_Sanatorium`) as a fifth location,
  "Arena: Iceberg": a multi-storey hotel with a rotunda on a rocky canyon, with its lobby, atrium, pool hall and cafe inside
  and a street area around it. It uses Arena's CheckPoint layout: two team pockets behind sliding gates at the east and west
  ends and three capture points, A, B and C, between them. The map has Arena's own scenes, materials and colliders, a
  newly baked NavMesh for bots, Arena's room acoustics and ambient sounds, and two exits (an east and a west gate). No loot
  and no scav raids, as on every map. What Iceberg has:
  - Arena's gate and door sounds for its sliding gates (three clips, played when a mode plugin releases held gates), Arena's
    door and gate end positions (11 rows) and TV screens that can show a mode's timer and scores through the ScoreMonitors
    integration (6 holder rows, 4 of them with a face). Like the other maps, these do nothing in an ordinary raid.
  - The sea below the south promenade is drawn with EFT's native water, as Bay5's harbor is.
  - A DynamicMaps overview of the ground level (the folder `dynamicmaps/ArenaMapPort_Iceberg` inside the plugin folder,
    for DynamicMaps users).
  - Lamps: the map has emissive materials far brighter than any other map's. Instead of lowering the bloom for the whole map,
    the 5 brightest materials are held to a ceiling of 4.8 and 15 dim lamps are lifted to a floor of 1.9 once per raid (37
    emissive materials in all), and the original values are restored when the raid ends. The two numbers were chosen by the
    author by eye.
  - Loading screens and a menu banner of its own.
  - What it does not have: **no spectator crowd** (Arena's Iceberg has none), so no calls, no murmur, no roar layer and no
    reactions; **no per-light lamp flares** (Equator, Bay5 and Bowl have them). Grass and the wind in the trees are not ported,
    and there is no snow. Arena's files carry no official name or description text for the map, so its description is the
    port's own.
  - Provisional: the ambient sound mix (3 dB, offset 0, range 2), the level of the lamp loops, the gate-sound level (-0.9 dB)
    and the lighting ZeroLevel (-3.7) are start values; the sounds have not been listened to yet. Two of the six TV holders stay dark. The olive
    tree at the cafe and the canyon rocks were not looked at in game; their shaders resolve in the log without an error.
- **Crowd reactions (for mode plugins).** The public class `ArenaMapPort.Client.SpectatorCrowd` now has eight members
  (`ContractVersion` is still 1; the four older members are unchanged). A mode plugin can claim the reactions and ask the
  spectators to answer an event: Approve, Disapprove, TakeCover, Start and Finish, each with a strength. They use 70 of Arena's
  own fan reaction clips, exported from Arena's files. One to three spectators answer through the same spatial sound as the calls,
  and the random calls pause for a moment afterwards. **Nothing reacts in a plain raid, and ArenaMode does not use the contract
  yet**: the reactions play only through a mode plugin or the developer test `Spectators/ReactionDemo` (needs
  `General/DeveloperMode`). The kinds Lull and PurifiedTeam are reserved and not implemented. There is no player setting.
- **Roar layer.** A reaction also lets the stands roar: Approve, Start and Finish play one of Arena's three Cheer crowd beds,
  Disapprove one of the three Angry beds, TakeCover none, as looping sources from every group of spectators with a 0.3-second
  attack, a hold of 1.5 to 5 seconds and a 2-second release. The six recordings are exported from Arena's files like the other
  crowd audio. It exists only where a crowd exists (four maps), and only when a reaction is asked for.
- **The crowd murmur is Arena's neutral in-match recording.** The murmur of 0.4.0 was Arena's post-match recording; since
  2 November it is the "neutral" crowd bed of Arena's in-match sounds (79.5 seconds, mono, with a smoothed loop seam, brought
  to the level of the 0.4.0 murmur). Arena's maps carry this recording on emitters that are inactive in the imported scenes, so
  playing it from the stands is the port's choice, not a copy of how Arena plays it in a match. The author compared the two
  recordings and chose the neutral one. A reaction does not lift the murmur; the roar layer plays Arena's Cheer and Angry beds instead (the lift of an earlier unreleased build was removed).

- **Arena installation check: a missing launcher uninstall stub is tolerated.** A player's antivirus quarantined `Uninstall.exe`
  (the BSG launcher's uninstall stub at the root of the Arena folder) after every reinstall, and 0.4.0 refused the whole installation
  with `ARENA_INCOMPLETE` (`1 of 15,351 files ... Uninstall.exe: missing`) although the maps never read that file. That one file may
  now be missing or of any size; every other listed file is still required with its listed size, and a tolerated absence is named in
  the `[Arena]` log line (`..., Uninstall.exe missing (tolerated)`). A fixed exception like the existing BattlEye and `Logging.config`
  size tolerances, not a setting.

The Equator static fix, the crowd murmur itself, the 4-to-8-second pauses between spectator calls, the gate and door sounds, the
score screens, the animated crowds and the puddle footsteps shipped in 0.4.0 and are not new. The in-game map notices and the
Arena installation check are unchanged.

## Crowd cost

Unchanged from 0.4.0, for the four maps that have a crowd (Iceberg has none and pays nothing): the crowds decode 123 shared images
into about 0.5 GB of video memory per raid (calculated from the image sizes: uncompressed, with mip levels), and building a crowd
adds about two seconds to raid loading. There is no setting for the crowds. To skip the crowd of one map, close EFT and delete the
folder `BepInEx/plugins/maschine-ArenaMapPort/spectators/<map_key>` (`arena_equator`, `arena_bay5`, `arena_airpit` or `arena_bowl`;
Equator day and night share `arena_equator`). The next raid on that map logs a warning that the crowd could not be restored (it can
appear twice, because EFT builds the spawn points twice) and continues without the crowd, its calls, its murmur and its reactions.
Keep the `spectators/shared` folder and the other maps' folders. Extracting the release ZIP over the installation again brings the
deleted folder back.

## Requirements and compatibility

Tested with **SPT 4.1.6 / EFT 0.16.9.5-40743**; the server declares `~4.1.5`. Both client and server
components are required. An installed **Escape from Tarkov: Arena** is required on the machines
loading/registering the maps. Encrypted bundle keys target Arena **0.6.1.0.48206** and were verified on
**0.6.2.0.48473** for all five maps; the four maps of 0.4.0 were also verified on **0.6.0.0.47752** (that check was not run
for Iceberg). An Arena update that changes the sampled map resources needs updated keys.
This requirement is not copy protection; art, audio and supporting data beside the bundles stay plaintext.

ArenaMode supplies match rules and respawns separately. DynamicMaps (tested with 1.2.1) and SAIN are optional.
Fika compatibility has not been verified. No loot or scav raids are included.

## Installation

1. Install **Escape from Tarkov: Arena** with the Battlestate Games launcher if it is not installed
   yet. It is a separate purchase from Escape from Tarkov.
2. Close EFT and the SPT server.
3. Extract the whole `maschine-ArenaMapPort-0.5.0.zip` into your SPT installation (the folder that contains `BepInEx`
   and `SPT_Runtime`), merging both folders and replacing existing ArenaMapPort files. Install both the client and
   server components.
4. Start the SPT server, then EFT.

The client folder is `BepInEx/plugins/maschine-ArenaMapPort`; its `keys` subfolder belongs to the release, so take it
from the same ZIP when you update (a missing or damaged key file is reported as an error in the client log, and the
maps do not load).
The server folder remains `SPT_Runtime/user/mods/ArenaMapPort`.

Arena is installed but not found? Set its folder in `General / ArenaInstallPath` (client,
`BepInEx/config/com.maschine.ArenaMapPort.cfg`) and in `SPT_Runtime/user/mods/ArenaMapPort/config/arena-install.json`
(server, `{"installPath": "D:\\Battlestate Games\\Escape from Tarkov Arena"}`, backslashes doubled);
both are checked like the automatic path.

**Upgrading from 0.4.0:** with EFT and the SPT server closed, extract the ZIP over the installation and keep
`BepInEx/config`. The ZIP replaces the client and the server DLL together (a running SPT server keeps its DLL
locked, so close it first) and adds Iceberg's files: its three encrypted bundles, `keys/arena_iceberg.json`, its shader
sidecar, acoustics, gate sounds, water, DynamicMaps overview, load screens and banner, and its server data under
`SPT_Runtime/user/mods/ArenaMapPort/db/arena_iceberg`. In `spectator-audio` it adds the 70 reaction clips, the six roar beds
(`arena_fans_ambient_cheer*.wav`, `arena_fans_ambient_angry*.wav`) and the neutral murmur
(`arena_fans_ambient_neutral.wav`), and replaces `spectator-audio.json`. The murmur recording of 0.4.0,
`arena_fans_ambient_postmatch.wav`, stays in the folder after the update: the client reads only the file the manifest names, so
it is ignored and may be deleted. There are no configuration changes and no new player settings (the four public
settings are unchanged). A crowd folder you deleted under `spectators` comes back when the ZIP is extracted; delete it again
afterwards if you still want that map without its crowd.

**Upgrading from 0.3.0:** the same, and the ZIP also replaces the Equator scenes bundle with the repaired one (the file
name is the same, so it overwrites 0.3.0's) and adds the `spectators`, `gate-sounds` and `monitors` folders.
`BepInEx/plugins/maschine-ArenaMapPort/parts/full.json`, the manifest of the previous ZIP, is replaced by the manifest of this ZIP.

**Upgrading from 0.2.3 or older:** the same. Every bundle has been encrypted since 0.3.0 and comes with a `keys`
folder, so the two DLLs alone are not enough. From 0.2.1 or older, the old
`BepInEx/plugins/maschine-ArenaMapPort/material-shaders.json` may be deleted (Equator's shader sidecar is now
`material-shaders.arena_equator.json`).

**Upgrading a development build:** move the old `BepInEx/plugins/ArenaMapPort` directory outside
`BepInEx/plugins` before extracting, and keep your configuration files. Remove the retired
`maschine-ArenaMapDebug.dll` if present, either directly in `plugins` or inside `plugins/ArenaMapDebug`.
The README.txt in the ZIP (`BepInEx/plugins/maschine-ArenaMapPort/README.txt`) covers old database/DynamicMaps
cleanup.

## Verification and known limitations

What was run, by whom and when. These are bounded checks of the builds named, not a certificate.

- **Checks on the build machine, 7 November 2026:** the distribution validator passed for all five maps (15 bundles, all
  encrypted), the encryption keys of all five maps unlock on Arena 0.6.2.0.48473, Iceberg and Bowl passed the port
  validator, and the bundle-block check (the check behind the Equator static fix) passes on the distribution. The .NET test suite
  (3,184 tests) and the Python suite passed on the working tree of the roar-layer change. Final gates on the release
  build (commit `a18ecd7`, 7 November): client and server Release builds with 0 warnings, 3,202 .NET tests and the Python suite (2,886 tests)
  passed, DLL versions 0.5.0 in source, projects and the staged binaries, the distribution validator, the bundle-name check, the port validator for Iceberg and Bowl,
  the 359-file plan; the ZIP was cut from that commit, extracted and compared file by file with the staged distribution (359 identical), and verified against its checksum.
- **Iceberg on the test host (GPU-2), 30 October to 7 November:** raids without bots and with Medium bots, the NavMesh
  (18,703 vertices, 8,599 triangles) installed, routes from the spawn pocket to all three capture points, the other pocket and both
  exits complete, a walk through the east pocket's sliding-gate doorway, bots patrolling, about 120 frames per second, no ArenaMapPort warning, the hideout
  opened afterwards. The native sea, the lamp ceiling and floor, the overview and its markers were each checked there by log or
  screenshot (the last emission line, on 7 November, read 5 capped and 15 lifted at 4.8 / 1.9; a raid on the 0.5.0 build:
  on 7 November, 18:32-18:37, two Iceberg raids with the 0.5.0 build: the same emission line (5 capped, 15 lifted, 23 ms), a 20-second developer gate hold
  released on time with the gates driven open in 2.5 s and four gate sounds playing, a spawn in the west pocket and an extraction at the Iceberg West Gate ("Early extraction",
  "Run Through" after 31 s), and a raid with Medium bots in which two PMCs and a Scav spawned, moved on the NavMesh under SAIN and fought (one bot killed another); 120 fps, no warning).
- **Not run for Iceberg:** a raid as a player from the west pocket, a bot raid longer than a few minutes, an actual extraction
  at either exit, and a gate hold and release with moving doors. Bot behaviour in a real match is up to ArenaMode.
- **Not listened to:** Iceberg's ambient sounds, its lamp loops and its gate sounds. The gate and door sounds of the other
  maps were checked in the log only as well. Iceberg's lamp limits (4.8 and 1.9) were tuned by the author by eye on his own PC. Its
  sea, the other materials and the remaining lamps were checked on the test host by log lines and screenshots only; no separate judgement
  of them by the author is recorded. Iceberg's menu banner and loading screens are shipped but were not looked at in game.
- **Crowd reactions:** the 70 clips and the five kinds ran on GPU-2 on 2 November on Equator, Bay5, Air Pit and Bowl with
  the developer demo, without a warning (Iceberg correctly reports no kinds). The author listened to the demo cycle (reaction
  voices with the roar beds) on 7 November and said it sounds good. That is a judgement of the demo, not a review of each clip,
  and it did not say whether the boos read separately. The roar layer ran on GPU-2 on 7 November on Equator and Bowl only
  (120 fps, no warning); Bay5 and Air Pit were not run, and the cut of one roar by the other kind and the retrigger of
  the same kind are covered by unit tests only. **ArenaMode has not adopted the contract**, so no match event drives a
  reaction yet.
- **Neutral murmur:** it ran on GPU-2 on 2 November without a warning; the author chose it after listening to the export of
  Arena's crowd recordings. The level approved on 30 October was for the old recording; the new one is brought to the same
  loudness and was not listened to again on the host.
- Gate and door sounds: Bay5's were never in range during a test, and the loudness, reach and fit of the clips shortened
  to the faster gate motion are untuned. The exit lamps were checked by reading their state, not by eye.
- Puddle footsteps: Equator's puddle cache and the rain-driven puddles have not been checked in game, and the splash sound has
  not been listened to on any map.
- A faint steady hiss and short clicks seen in one recording of Equator (before 0.4.0) are not explained by the static fix;
  the author confirmed in play on 30 October that the static itself is gone.
- EFT's own sound bundle has no impact sounds for the Books and CardBoard prop types. On Equator 39 props
  (30 Books: shoes, books, blue cups and a road cone; 9 CardBoard: pizza boxes and office folders), and any
  props of those types on other maps, stay silent when they hit something.
- Fika compatibility has not been verified. There is no loot and no scav raid on any map.
